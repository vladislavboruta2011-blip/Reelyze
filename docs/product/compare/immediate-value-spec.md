# Compare Immediate-Value Product Specification

**Status:** Internal working specification
**Evidence status:** Product hypothesis awaiting creator validation
**Scope:** Current-script decision support, not long-term learning

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Compare is a current-script decision-support tool.

Its immediate job is to help a creator examine one concrete moment in their own script against one real alternative used in another published script.

The product does not need to prove long-term learning to be valuable.

Its smallest complete value unit is:

1. the creator's real script moment;
2. a real alternative execution from another published script;
3. a grounded explanation of the difference;
4. an open applicability decision left to the creator.

Compare is not positioned as:

- a winner detector;
- a performance predictor;
- a rewrite engine;
- a permanent learning system;
- proof of why a competitor succeeded.

---

## 2. Job to Be Done

**Primary job:** "When I am uncertain about how to handle a specific moment in my Shorts script, show me one real, relevant alternative and help me decide whether anything from it belongs in my own version."

- **Triggering situation:** the creator is unsure whether a specific moment in their script (hook, transition, payoff, etc.) is the right choice, and has no easy way to see an alternative on their own.
- **Creator input:** their own script, or the specific moment within it.
- **Creator decision:** adapt, reject, investigate further, or leave unchanged.
- **Successful outcome (observable in the current session):** the creator identifies a concrete difference, understands the evidence behind it, makes an adapt/reject/investigate decision, and can state what they will do next.
- **Unsuccessful outcome:** the creator cannot locate a concrete difference, doesn't trust or understand the evidence, or leaves without a decision.

Success is defined entirely within the session. Long-term transfer is not required for the job to be done.

---

## 3. Core Product Promise

**Public-facing:** "See how another published Short handles the same craft moment — then decide what, if anything, is worth adapting for yours."

**Internal formulation:** Compare gives the creator one real, independently authored execution of the same structural moment they're uncertain about, so the decision about their own script is grounded in a concrete comparison rather than intuition alone.

Avoided in both formulations: "what works," "why it performed," "learn from successful videos," "copy winning techniques," "improve retention," "become a better writer." Any framing implying the alternative is stronger, correct, or causally responsible for performance is out of bounds.

---

## 4. The Minimum Value Unit

**A. Your moment** — exact excerpt from the creator's script.

**B. Real alternative** — exact excerpt from another published script, with timestamp where available.

**C. Grounded difference** — descriptive explanation of what each script does. No outcome claim.

**D. Applicability decision** — adapt, reject, investigate further, or leave unchanged.

One instance of this unit is sufficient to create value. The product does not need to surface many findings to be worth using — a single well-grounded moment, understood and decided on, closes the loop the job-to-be-done describes. Volume of findings is not the value driver; the presence of one concrete, decidable comparison is.

The second script must remain visibly and legibly *real* — sourced from an actual published video, inspectable by the creator — because the entire value of the comparison depends on the creator understanding that what they're looking at is something someone else genuinely did, not a generated suggestion. If the alternative doesn't read as real, the creator has no way to know what the comparison bought them over a synthetic suggestion, and the product collapses into an unlabeled rewrite engine.

---

## 5. Information Order

The correct conceptual sequence:

1. Anchor the creator's own moment.
2. Show the real alternative.
3. Explain the observed difference.
4. Explain the alternative's craft logic without calling it better.
5. Ask whether it fits the creator's script.
6. Optionally surface a caution against blind copying.

Evidence precedes interpretation at every step. This section describes sequencing of information only, not layout, components, or visual treatment.

---

## 6. Role of the Competitor Script

**What it contributes today:**

- an independently authored, published execution of the same moment;
- a concrete alternative rather than a generic suggestion;
- exact source material the creator can inspect directly;
- a bounded reference sufficient for one decision.

**What it does not prove:**

- that the alternative caused any performance outcome;
- that the alternative is superior;
- that it fits the creator's voice or format;
- that it should be copied;
- that the competitor's overall script is better.

"Competitor" is a useful framing at entry — it explains where the second script came from and why it's worth looking at. Once inside the result, the creator's own script remains the subject; the competitor script is reference material, not the thing being evaluated.

---

## 7. Analyze vs. Compare

**Analyze** examines one script using general craft reasoning. It identifies concerns, strengths, and possible revisions.

**Compare** adds one real, independently authored execution. It makes a concrete alternative inspectable and supports a decision grounded in two real sources rather than one script plus commentary.

This is not a claim that Analyze can never generate a useful alternative — it can suggest what could be done. The current, supportable distinction is narrower:

> Analyze can suggest what could be done. Compare can show what another published script actually did.

---

## 8. Primary and Secondary Value

**Primary value (supported today):**

- current-script clarity;
- a concrete alternative to weigh;
- a faster, bounded decision;
- reduced uncertainty around one creative choice.

**Secondary possible value — unvalidated repeated-use hypothesis, each item labeled as such:**

- noticing recurring personal defaults — *unvalidated repeated-use hypothesis*;
- expanding perceived options over time — *unvalidated repeated-use hypothesis*;
- developing broader craft intuition — *unvalidated repeated-use hypothesis*;
- future independent transfer to unseen scripts — *unvalidated repeated-use hypothesis*.

None of these secondary items may constrain the current product flow, gate a design decision, or appear in marketing copy until tested per the validation plan (Section 15).

---

## 9. Product Principles

1. Current-script utility before learning theory.
2. One concrete decision is enough to create value.
3. The creator's script remains primary; the competitor script is reference.
4. The other script is evidence, not authority.
5. Show exact source material — no paraphrased-only evidence.
6. Explain choices; never declare winners.
7. Encourage judgment, not copying.
8. Do not add friction solely for speculative educational benefit.
9. Do not obscure why Compare differs from Analyze.
10. Honest failure (no usable comparison) is better than unsupported advice.

---

## 10. Claim and Copy Boundaries

**Approved now:**

- "See one real alternative used in another published script."
- "Examine how two scripts handle the same craft moment differently."
- "Decide what, if anything, is worth adapting."
- "Review exact excerpts and timestamps."

**Allowed only after validation:**

- "Build broader creative range."
- "Recognize your recurring defaults."
- "Generate more structural alternatives independently."
- "Develop lasting creative intuition."

**Forbidden:**

- causal claims;
- performance predictions;
- winner language;
- "this worked";
- "this made the video successful";
- "your script is weaker because…";
- "copy this technique";
- unsupported claims about editing, delivery, audience, or retention.

---

## 11. Current Contract Fit

Mapping the existing Compare contract fields to the immediate-value model:

| Field | Role in immediate value | Essential / Secondary | Presentation risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dimension findings | Names which craft moment is being compared (hook, transition, payoff…) | Essential | Low — this is the anchor for the whole unit |
| User evidence | The creator's own exact excerpt (Component A) | Essential | Low |
| Competitor evidence + timestamps | The real alternative and its source (Component B) | Essential | Medium — must stay visibly real and inspectable, not summarized away |
| Observations | The grounded, descriptive difference (Component C) | Essential | Medium — risk of drifting into evaluative language ("better") rather than descriptive |
| Conclusions | Framing that supports the applicability decision (Component D) | Essential | High — most likely field to smuggle in outcome/performance claims if not held to the boundaries in Section 10 |
| Priorities | Helps the creator decide what to act on first if multiple moments are surfaced | Secondary | Medium — useful for triage, but not part of the minimum value unit; risks implying ranking equals importance beyond the current script |
| Cautions | Prevents blind copying, supports the "investigate" and "reject" branches | Secondary but load-bearing | Low, if kept short and specific |
| Summary | Rolls up the session | Secondary | High — a summary that restates everything risks becoming the "dense AI report" failure mode (Section 14) instead of reinforcing one decidable unit |

This section maps existing fields to the model above; it does not propose changes to the contract.

---

## 12. Product Experience Requirements

Described as requirements, not components:

- the creator should be able to identify something concrete quickly;
- the two exact moments (theirs and the alternative's) must be easy to inspect side by side;
- the competitor's contribution must be obvious, not buried in prose;
- interpretation must not bury the underlying evidence;
- the result must not feel like a dense AI report;
- multiple findings may exist, but each single unit must be understandable independently of the others;
- priorities must support a decision, not repeat the analysis already given;
- cautions must actively work against blind borrowing, not just disclaim it;
- the result must preserve the creator's inputs and recover gracefully after errors.

---

## 13. Success Metrics

Immediate, behavioral, observable within a single session:

- identifies a specific difference between the two moments;
- refers to exact evidence (excerpt or timestamp) rather than a vague impression;
- names something worth adapting, rejecting, or investigating;
- states a concrete next action;
- can correctly state that no winner or performance result was proven;
- names a real workflow moment where they'd use this again;
- returns to use Compare again on another real script.

Long-term skill transfer is explicitly excluded as a launch metric.

---

## 14. Failure Modes

| Failure mode | Classification |
|---|---|
| Result reads as generic AI advice | Product-value problem |
| Competitor adds no perceived value beyond Analyze | Product-value problem |
| Creator cannot locate the evidence | Information-architecture problem |
| Creator treats the "stronger side" language as a declared winner | Copy/trust problem |
| Creator copies without considering fit | Copy/trust problem |
| Excessive text hides the actionable difference | Information-architecture problem |
| Product gives advice unsupported by either source | Model-output problem |
| Creator wants only a rewrite | Product-value problem |
| No usable transcript from the competitor source | Technical limitation |
| Safe validation rejection after both attempts | Technical limitation |

---

## 15. Validation Plan

**Primary question:** "Does Compare help creators make a concrete current-script decision that they could not make as easily from Analyze alone?"

**Observe:**

- what catches attention first;
- whether a real alternative is identified as distinct;
- whether evidence is actually inspected (not just skimmed past);
- what decision changes as a result;
- whether the creator adapts, rejects, or investigates;
- whether the creator perceives a winner or causal claim that wasn't made.

Long-term transfer requires a separate future study (per the falsifiable validation plan already scoped) and must not be inferred from these sessions.

---

## 16. Decision Rules

- **A — Immediate value is strong:** continue refining the result experience around concrete decisions.
- **B — Value exists but is hidden:** reduce density and strengthen the evidence/action hierarchy.
- **C — Users only value priorities:** consider repositioning around decision support rather than broad comparison.
- **D — Competitor adds little beyond Analyze:** rethink the cost and role of the second script.
- **E — Winner/causal misunderstanding repeats:** stop expansion and fix trust language first.
- **F — Users mainly copy blindly:** strengthen fit questions and cautions.

---

## 17. Explicit Non-Goals

This specification does not commit Climpy to:

- full-script annotations;
- browser extension work;
- YouTube Analytics integration;
- Compare History;
- long-term learning infrastructure;
- spaced practice;
- personalized curriculum;
- automated rewriting;
- performance forecasting;
- proving why a video succeeded.

---

## 18. Open Questions

- How many value units should be visible initially?
- Should the result begin with the biggest difference or the clearest actionable one?
- How visible must the competitor source be for it to read as legitimately real?
- How much reasoning is necessary before the creator can decide?
- When does a caution deserve equal prominence to the finding itself?
- Does "Compare" remain the best entry-point name?
- What result presentation best avoids the dense-report failure mode?

These remain open and should not be resolved without creator evidence.

---

## 19. Current Product Stance

Compare should currently be built and marketed as a grounded decision-support tool for the creator's current script.

Its immediate value does not depend on proving permanent skill transfer.

Long-term learning remains a separate internal hypothesis that may later become a product advantage — only after behavioral validation.
