# The Compare Constitution

An internal product document, not marketing and not a vision statement. It exists so future engineers and designers can evaluate a feature proposal against what Compare is actually for, rather than against what would be interesting to build.

## Purpose

A creator writing a script has exactly one data point: their own draft. They have no way to test an alternative before publishing, so every creative decision is made on instinct, imitation of vague "best practices," or faith. Compare exists to give a creator a second, real data point — an actual script, by an actual creator, that made a different decision at the same narrative moment — so a creative choice can be weighed against something real instead of decided alone.

## Core Transformation

**Before Compare:** *"The only script I can really examine is my own — any other approach is just a theory until I publish it and see what happens."*

**After Compare:** *"I can point to a real, specific choice someone else actually made at the same moment in their script — not a theory, an actual instance — and I understand the reasoning behind it clearly enough to judge for myself whether it belongs in mine."*

Every feature, every sentence of copy, every design decision Compare ever makes should be traceable back to producing this shift. If it doesn't move a creator from the first belief to the second, it isn't part of the product — it's decoration on top of it.

## Non-negotiable Principles

**Every claim must be grounded in the real scripts.** An excerpt must be an exact, verifiable quote from a real source; a timestamp must correspond to a real moment in a real transcript. This is not a quality bar — it is the entire mechanism that makes Compare trustworthy instead of merely articulate. Remove it and Compare becomes a chatbot with better formatting.

**Never imply causality or performance we cannot prove.** Compare can prove a choice is real. It cannot prove a choice caused a result. No language may suggest a script "worked," "performed better," or that applying an insight will produce a specific outcome. This line does not move for better copywriting.

**Return the final creative judgment to the creator.** Compare explains and shows. It never instructs a creator to copy, never rewrites their script for them, and never presents its own reasoning as a verdict rather than an input to someone else's decision.

**Compare creative decisions, not people.** The subject of every finding is a choice in a document — a line, a beat, a transition — never a judgment about which creator is more talented, more successful, or better at their craft. There are no winners.

**The competitor is a teacher, not just evidence.** A real excerpt without an honest account of why it's a coherent creative choice is just a citation. Every reference to the competitor's script must carry the reasoning behind the choice, not merely the fact of it.

**Prefer one meaningful insight over many shallow ones.** A comparison that tries to cover every possible dimension with equal weight teaches nothing memorably. Find the one thing that actually matters for this specific pair of scripts and go deep on it before considering anything else.

**Ceremony must never outrun substance.** Interaction design, visualization, and pacing exist only to make a grounded insight clearer or faster to absorb. The moment a device is justified by "it's memorable" or "it's engaging" rather than "it makes the real claim easier to trust or understand," it is decoration, and decoration is always optional.

## What Compare Is NOT

- **Not another AI report.** A stack of findings with better prose is still a report. Compare fails if it can be mistaken for one.
- **Not a script generator.** It never writes or rewrites the creator's script. It points, explains, and stops.
- **Not a prediction engine.** It never forecasts views, retention, virality, or any future outcome.
- **Not a performance guarantee.** Nothing it says implies a specific result if its suggestions are followed.
- **Not a teaching course.** Learning is a side effect of answering a real, urgent question quickly, never a curriculum the creator is expected to complete.
- **Not a collection of clever visualizations.** Novelty and shareability are not goals. A visualization earns its place only by making a grounded claim faster or clearer to trust.
- **Not a competition or a scoreboard.** No scores, no rankings, no declared winner.
- **Not a substitute for the creator's judgment.** It informs a decision. It never makes one.

## Relationship to Analyze

Analyze has exactly one data point: the creator's own script. Everything it says is reasoned from general craft knowledge, because it has no real second case to point to — it can flag a risk but can never show a real, working alternative. Compare exists precisely because that is a hard ceiling, not a gap Analyze can eventually close by getting smarter. Compare's entire value depends on having two real, verified scripts in front of it; Analyze's value depends on not requiring one. Neither replaces the other because they answer different questions — "what's true of my script" versus "what does a real alternative look like, and why." Do not attempt to give Analyze comparison behavior, and do not attempt to make Compare useful without a second real script. Both erase the reason each exists.

## Relationship to ChatGPT

A general-purpose chat model can produce fluent, confident-sounding comparative commentary about two pasted scripts with almost no effort, in whatever tone or depth is asked for — that is a real strength, and Compare should not try to out-write it. What it cannot guarantee is that any given claim is actually, verifiably true of the text in front of it. It has no obligation to ground a statement in an exact excerpt, no mechanism preventing an invented detail that merely sounds plausible, and no built-in resistance to sliding from "here's a technique" into "so copy it" or "so this performs better." Compare is built around exactly the constraints a chat interface doesn't have by default: mandatory grounding, a validator that rejects invented facts and causal claims, and a hard rule that the decision to adopt anything stays with the creator. Compare is not trying to be a better conversationalist. It is trying to be a source that can actually be trusted, which is a narrower and harder target.

## Future Design Filter

Before adding anything to Compare, answer:

- Does this strengthen the core transformation — a real example, honest reasoning, and the creator's own judgment?
- Does it increase trust, or does it only increase engagement?
- Is every claim it makes grounded in the real scripts?
- Does it help the creator make a better decision, or does it just make the product more impressive to look at?
- Does it avoid implying causality, performance, or a guarantee we cannot back?
- Is it worth the added complexity, given that one deep insight beats many shallow ones?
- Could a busy creator still get the honest core answer in under two minutes without this feature?

If the answer is no to most of these, the feature does not belong here, regardless of how interesting, well-designed, or technically impressive it is.

## Closing Principle

Compare's job is to hand a creator one real, verifiable reference point for one real decision, explained honestly enough that they can judge it for themselves, as quickly as respecting their time requires. That is the whole product. Everything else — how it looks, how it's paced, how memorable it feels — is optional, and every option defaults to no unless it can show, specifically, that it makes this one thing more real, more honest, or faster to reach.
